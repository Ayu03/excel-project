# Excel Project Report: Superstore Sales Analysis

## Executive Summary

This project analyzes the **Corporate customer segment** of the Superstore Sales dataset to identify profitable and loss-making product subcategories across different regions and provinces. Using Microsoft Excel features such as filtering, sorting, conditional formatting, and Pivot Tables, the dataset was transformed into a structured report to support business decision-making.

The analysis identified the top-performing and least-performing product subcategories, highlighted regional variations in profitability, and uncovered patterns that could influence inventory planning and product portfolio decisions. An anomaly was also observed between the performance of complementary products, leading to hypotheses for further business investigation.

Based on these findings, the report recommends prioritizing investigation into high-loss categories, maintaining focus on consistently profitable products, and adopting region-specific inventory and product strategies. Overall, the project demonstrates how Excel can be used not only for data organization and reporting but also to generate actionable business insights from sales data.

## Business Problem

Superstore's management wanted to identify which product subcategories were generating consistent profits and which were contributing to significant losses within the Corporate customer segment. The goal was to understand regional and provincial performance so that inventory, pricing, and product portfolio decisions could be made more effectively.

## Dataset Overview

The dataset contains transactional sales records for a Canadian retail superstore, including order details, sales, profit, customer segment, product category, product subcategory, geographic information (Region and Province), shipping details, and product margins. For this analysis, only the Corporate customer segment was considered.

## Objectives

- Identify the highest and lowest performing product subcategories.
- Compare profitability across regions and provinces.
- Highlight top-performing sales orders.
- Identify consistently major loss-making products.
- Generate recommendations to improve profitability.

## Excel Features Used

- Advanced Sorting and Filtering: organize data.
- Conditional Formatting: highlight trends under profit.
- Pivot Tables: summarize profitability/loss.
- Number Formatting: improve readability.
- Freeze Panes: easier navigation.
- Borders and Layout: reporting.

## Methodology

1. Filtered the dataset to the Corporate customer segment.
2. Cleaned and formatted the data.
3. Organized records by Region and Province.
4. Applied conditional formatting to highlight sales and profit patterns.
5. Built Pivot Tables to analyze profitability & losses by product subcategory & Region.
6. Identified top-performing and loss-making subcategories.
7. Interpreted findings to derive business recommendations.

## Key Findings

*[Observation -> Insight -> Business Implication]*

- **Office Machines** and **Telephones & Communication,** followed by **Binders and Binder Accessories,** consistently appear among the top three most profitable product subcategories across almost all regions.

  This suggests stable demand and strong financial performance of these subcategories.

  Management should ensure adequate inventory levels and continue monitoring demand for these categories. These categories may represent opportunities for continued investment and promotional focus.

- **Tables** generate significant losses in Ontario, whereas **Pens & Art Supplies** remain profitable in the same region.

  Product performance is highly region-specific. This indicates that product profitability depends not only on the product itself but also on regional demand and market conditions.

  Management should keep a close watch on all regional demands/issues, especially since this is a major factor in the final loss/profit mandate for the subcategories.

- **Tables** recorded the highest overall losses among all product subcategories.

  Losses are concentrated in a single product category rather than being evenly distributed across all products.

  This warrants further investigation into pricing, discount strategies, shipping costs, product returns, or regional demand before making inventory decisions.

- While categories such as **Rubber Bands** incur only minor losses, **Tables** contribute substantially larger losses.

  Loss-making categories vary considerably in magnitude.

  This indicates that management should prioritize high-impact categories first.

- **Chairs** are profitable, whereas **Tables** are among the highest loss-making product subcategories.

  This is an unusual pattern because these products are often complementary purchases. Customers may not be purchasing Tables at the same rate due to factors such as pricing, limited variety, design preferences, inventory availability, or regional demand differences.

## Business Recommendations

*[Corresponding to business implications given in the Key Findings section]*

*[Business Implication -> Business Recommendation]*

- Increase inventory of consistently profitable products, also focus on promotional marketing of these subcategories.
- Monitor regional performance quarterly.
- Review pricing and discount strategies, shipping costs, etc. specifically for Tables. Investigate the causes of losses, optimize pricing or supply chain where possible, and only then decide whether to reduce or discontinue the subcategory.
- Prioritize investigation and corrective action for the highest loss-making category (Tables) before allocating resources to lower-impact categories. Analyze pricing, discount strategy, procurement costs, shipping costs, demand patterns, and regional performance to identify the root causes of the losses.
- Investigate why Tables are underperforming despite Chairs being profitable, even though these products are commonly complementary purchases. Analyze customer purchase patterns, pricing, product assortment, inventory availability, and regional demand. If supported by the findings, consider testing bundle offers, expanding complementary product options, or optimizing the product mix.
